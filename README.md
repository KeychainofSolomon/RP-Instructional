# RP-Instructional

Skills for roleplay agents. Install one, and the agent running your scenes follows it.

## npc-temperament

An engine for running non-player characters as people with lives of their own, in any setting
(fantasy, near-future, modern, historical, anything). It decides who each character is, what they
want, what happened to them today, how they treat the people in front of them, how they react
when things go wrong, and how they change over time. It does this with numbers and rules a
narrator can follow, then hides all of that behind plain, in-character prose.

It is built on three laws:

1. **Dice go upstream.** Dice decide what happens in a character's life: their history, their
   day, what they stumble on. How they react is then worked out from those causes. Nothing ever
   rolls "how she treats you".
2. **Nothing leaks.** The narrator never tells the player what their character feels or thinks,
   never states an NPC's inner life as fact, and no NPC knows what they couldn't know. Hidden
   layers show only as behaviour, details and objects.
3. **No quests without a drive.** NPCs rarely ask strangers for anything. A request for help
   only happens when five conditions line up, and even then the outsider can usually do only
   part of it.

## What it can do

### People, not quest-givers
- **Blended personalities.** Each character is made of one to eight of 20 temperament styles
  (Vigilant, Devoted, Schemer, Self-Made, Predator, Benevolent and more) in fractions:
  pure, three-quarters and a quarter, an even split, quarters, eighths. Seven traits come out of
  that blend with personal variance: Candour, Volatility, Energy, Warmth, Discipline, Openness,
  Dominance. On any given day, the part of them whose needs are hungriest leads.
- **Two ladders of needs.** Physical (Survival, Safety, Means, Comfort) and spiritual
  (Belonging, Standing, Selfhood, Meaning), each weighted for that person and tracked as Sated,
  Steady, Wanting or Starved. Most people put the body first; some will starve for a cause; some
  are fixated on one thing; some feel nothing pressing at all.
- **Drives.** About 10% want nothing in particular, 65% want something no outsider can give
  (to be forgiven by someone dead, to stop being afraid), and 25% want something an outsider
  could help with, usually only in part.
- **Masks.** Most characters are genuine. Some wear a face over who they are: masked, borrowed
  from someone else, shifting with each audience, hollow (performing feelings they don't have),
  or unanchored (certain of a past that may not be theirs). Masks slip under pressure, drop in
  pain, and can harden or soften over time.
- **Creeds, codes and compulsions.** What they hold highest (Strength, Mercy, Justice, Faith,
  Order, Kin and others, arranged so neighbours agree and opposites clash), the lines they won't
  cross, and the urges that build until they act on them.
- **Ties.** Family, lovers, mentors, rivals, enemies, masters, causes, places and objects,
  each with loyalty and reach. Ties can name a specific person, so relationships between
  characters are real.

### Every reaction has a cause
- **Their day.** Each in-world day rolls what happened to them, drawn from their own needs,
  ties and work ("fought with her sister", "the guild passed him over"), never repeating within a
  week.
- **How you treat them.** Eleven approaches (deference, flattery, bluntness, humour, pity,
  pressure and more), each scored from that person's traits. Flattery delights the vain and
  repels the sincere; pity moves the warm and makes the predatory lean in.
- **Trust** with a permanent part (big events) and a surface part (small ones that fade).
- **The room.** Whoever leads, whoever defers, whoever is their rival, moods that spread to the
  easily moved, people who play to an audience.
- **The scene.** Seven settings (Violence, Order, Scarcity, Strangeness, Faith, Power, Deceit)
  decide who appears, how hungry they are, how harsh their days are, how many masks there are.
- **Mood → band → form.** All of this adds up to a mood, which falls into a band (Hostile,
  Cool, Neutral, Warm, Kind). Each person shows that band in their own way: one gets Wary,
  another Appraising, another Gossipy. Bad days make most people curt, not cruel. Only some
  take it out on strangers.

### The moment changes how people come across
Nineteen **circumstances**, in priority order: hurt, frozen, afraid, enraged, stunned,
tending the wounded, protecting someone, grieving, unusually clear, sick, exhausted, drunk,
starving, solemn, festive, a superior watching, in private with someone trusted, in public,
on duty. Each one blocks what doesn't fit, turns it into what does, and exempts the natures
it doesn't touch. A shot man doesn't flirt. A gossip goes quiet at a funeral. Mockery in front
of the boss becomes silent contempt. The very dominant talk over their superiors anyway.
Circumstance also shapes speech, quirks, masks, which side of a blended person comes forward,
and how each approach lands. Results come with intensity and colour ("utterly Hostile, shows as
Grudge; does not move; held very still; silent, or a few words"), so the same person rarely
reads the same way twice.

### Violence with weight
- **Three registers**, set by the scene: *Rupture* (violence tears the ordinary), *Trade*
  (violence is business: courteous, euphemistic, often offscreen), *Weather* (violence as
  ordinary as weather, in long plain sentences).
- **Responses from relationships and processing.** Who was hurt (their child, their priest,
  their commander, a rival, an enemy, a stranger, a symbol of what they believe) and who did it
  decide the response, together with how this person processes things. Some act, some freeze,
  some go numb, some keep the old words, some refuse to believe it, some look after everyone
  else. Twenty-five responses in all: frantic, tending, shielding, covering for the one who did
  it, torn between two loved ones, obeying, watching to the end without helping, and more.
- **Dissonance from the place itself.** Each location has a sheet of what is always there (the
  prayer bell, the cats, the light through the shutters). Violent scenes draw their jarring
  details from it, least-used first, and the place keeps a mark afterwards (a stain that won't
  scrub out, a door that stays shut) that can surface again later.
- **A prose guide** for grit through exact physical detail and unexplained contrast, never by
  naming anyone's feelings.

### People change, slowly and for reasons
- **Memory.** Everyone on the scene remembers who they are and everything they lived through,
  unless dead, comatose or amnesiac by an established cause. Feelings fade; memories don't.
- **Pain and elation.** Every event is felt, scaled by closeness, sensitivity and familiarity,
  and split by how the person processes it: the expressive feel it now, the contained bottle it,
  the numb feel it days later.
- **Getting used to things.** The tenth sight of violence lands at an eighth of the first; joy
  wears thin the same way; forty quiet days and the soldier is sensitive again.
- **Clarity.** A rare, lucid neutrality that can only strike where different kinds of moments
  converge and pain and elation nearly cancel. Its margin widens with discipline and calm, and
  those who find it grow toward it.
- **Inclinations.** Whatever someone keeps doing becomes a pull, then a habit that fills quiet
  moments, then a compulsion with cravings. The same pleasure satisfies less over time.
- **Change against the anchor.** Traits move only when accumulated pressure outweighs the
  anchor of the person's underlying nature. A rooted trait survives a lot; someone they love can
  move it in two blows; an overwhelming moment moves it at once. A change held for three months
  becomes part of who they are. Nothing holds forever.
- **Grief** lasts according to who was lost (a child for weeks, a rival for days, an enemy not
  at all) and shows according to how they process it.

### Groups and the world
- **Circles** (households, crews, guilds, gangs, corporations) are built exactly like people:
  traits, voices, needs, creed, masks, days. Their good and bad days echo into their members.
- **Tides.** The scene settings drift week by week, pressing on circles, which press on people.
- **Conflict.** Hostile characters choose how to oppose someone from what they know: overwhelm,
  press, wait and probe, avoid battle and strike where it isn't expected, or yield.

## How it works, in one pass

```
the times (scene settings, drifting)
  └ the circle (same shape as a person; its days echo into members)
      └ the person (voices → traits; needs; ties; creed; mask; drive; history)
          └ their day (rolled: what happened to them, which voice leads, openness, temptation)
              └ the encounter (computed: mood from causes → band → form)
                  └ the moment (circumstances amend form, colour, speech, quirks, masks)
                      └ the prose (only what can be seen, heard and touched)

and afterwards: pain, elation, clarity, habit → pressure on traits → slow change → new self
```

The narrator runs the script (or follows the rules by hand), reads the result privately, and
writes the scene. Script output is never pasted into the story.

## Example

A guard called **Kasimir** (half Serious, half Vigilant; he processes things by going numb)
watches a guest shoot his friend Brother Anselm:

```
Circumstances: frozen, enraged, grieving
Band: utterly Hostile -> shows as Grudge (amended from Fear-Aggression)
Colour: does not move; holds whatever they were holding; held very still; every word measured
Speech: silent, or a few words
Quirks: feeds animals obsessively (not now)
```

In the story, that becomes something like: *Kasimir stood at the hearth with the boot in one
hand and the awl in the other, and he did not move, and he did not put either of them down.*
His grief arrives two days later. The warming room keeps a pale patch of scrubbed flagstone.

## Commands

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `npc` | Make a character (`--name`, `--dials`, `--save`). `--seeded` makes a name always give the same person. |
| `circle` | Make a group, from members or from scratch. |
| `place` | Make or extend a place sheet (`--add "sound:the prayer bell"`). |
| `tie` | Name a relationship (`--to "Bram" --role sibling --loyalty 5`). |
| `day` | Roll a character's or circle's day. |
| `meet` | Work out how a character treats someone right now, with every circumstance flag. |
| `ask` | Check whether a character would ask for help. |
| `event` | Record something that happened to them (betrayal, rescue, loss, reunion, joy and more). |
| `habit` | Record something they did again (drinking, praying, fighting, anything). |
| `violence` | Register, dissonance, aftermath and everyone's response; `--record` writes what they felt. |
| `scene` | Who leads the room, hidden developments, slips, oddities. |
| `affinity` | How two characters regard each other. |
| `status` | Dead, comatose, amnesiac, or back to present. |
| `tide` | Move the world a week on. |
| `audit` | Check every combination in the system (see below). |
| `styles`, `roll` | Reference tables; plain dice. |

## How it's tested

- **`audit`** checks every manner under every combination of up to three circumstances, in every
  mood band, across 35 personality profiles: over 6.5 million cases. It also checks all
  descriptive colour and contradiction text, every violence response for every pairing of
  relationships, and the behaviour of the change systems:
  - repeated pain weakens; familiarity fades again;
  - rooted traits resist moderate pushes; overwhelming events move them exactly one step;
  - held changes become part of the core;
  - habits grow by less each time, fade, and become exactly one compulsion;
  - a habit never breaks through a circumstance;
  - clarity never comes from pain alone and stays within its margin.

  Anything new added to the system must pass it before use.
- **Simulated campaigns:** 30 characters over 180 in-world days through every command and option
  (about 8,300 operations), with no errors and no unsettled results. Measured rates: about half
  a trait change per person in six busy months; about 7% of three-person scenes have a hidden
  development; a stranger is met with hostility less than 1% of the time without cause.
- **Story checks** of each system: soldiers getting used to violence, parents losing children,
  a jailer's mocking habit, a clerk's drinking, clarity across temperaments.

## Install

Download `npc-temperament.skill` and upload it under Skills in Claude's settings, or copy the
`npc-temperament/` folder into your skills directory. The script needs Python 3.8 or later and
nothing else. Without code execution, the skill's rules can be followed by hand, in a simplified
form described in `SKILL.md`.
