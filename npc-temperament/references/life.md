# Life: family, tastes, pets, outlook

## Contents
1. Idiosyncrasy (how far someone strays from their own grain)
2. Family
3. Tastes
4. Pets
5. Outlook on the unseen
6. Grief, by bond
7. In play

These layers stand beside temperament rather than flowing from it. A stern person can love
silly songs; a gentle one can loathe children's noise. Built once with the character, then canon.
Old ledgers gain them on load, worked out the same way every time from the person's id, and
gain no new ties.

## 1. Idiosyncrasy

How internally consistent this person is. Rolled, not derived: Consistent 15%, Ordinary 45%,
Marked 30%, Riddled 10% (one step up if Complete; always Riddled if an outlier).

| Level | Likes / dislikes | Against the grain | Contradictions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consistent** | 1 / 1 | never | none |
| **Ordinary** | 2 / 1–2 | possible | none |
| **Marked** | 3 / 2–3 | at least 1 | 40%: a paired contradiction; 50%: one relative who is the exception; 35%: an odd note in their outlook |
| **Riddled** | 4 / 3–4 | at least 2 | 80%, 80% and 70% for the same three |

## 2. Family

Everyone has one, even if it is all dead or never known. Separate from ties: ties are who
matters most; family is the whole of where they come from.

- **Feeling** (overall): Close, Dutiful, Strained, Scattered, Estranged, Broken. The unmoored
  lean toward Scattered and Estranged.
- **Members.** Two parents (alive, dead, or never known); 0–6 siblings with birth order (only
  child, eldest, middle, youngest, twin); a partner (none, spouse, lover, widowed, separated;
  the sworn rarely partner); 0–6 children, far more often with a spouse. Violence and scarcity
  make the dead more common. Each living or dead member has a **bond**: close, fond, dutiful,
  distant, strained, estranged, drawn from the family's feeling.
- **Why a bond went cold.** Every distant, strained or estranged parent, sibling, partner or
  child has a cause and a direction: who walked away (they did, the relative did, both, or no
  one; it just drifted). Parents can be deadbeats: walked out, never there, drank it away, only
  turn up when they want something. A parent never known can only have left; the dead don't
  turn up asking for money.
- **The exception.** In a Marked or Riddled person's family, one relative is the other way: the
  one warm sister in an estranged family, the one they can't stand in a close one.
- **Also:** who raised them if not their parents; their place in the family (the responsible
  one, the black sheep, the baby…); where they live and with whom; one thing handed down
  (a trade, a grudge, a saying, an heirloom); sometimes a family wound, which reshapes the bond
  it touches.
- **Dearest.** The living relative they love most, if any (a child first, then partner, sibling,
  parent). For new people the dearest becomes a tie, and generic kin ties are given a face from
  the family, so family turns up in their days. An estranged relative is never tied as beloved.

## 3. Tastes

Things they like and dislike: senses, food, people and manners, pastimes, things and places.
Each has a strength (likes / loves / delights in; dislikes / can't stand / loathes).

Some tastes lean toward a kind of person (crowds suit the energetic, routine the disciplined).
A taste held from the far other end of that trait is **against the grain**: shown, never
explained. **Paired contradictions** belong to one person: loves the sea, hates boats; loves
cooking for others, can't bear eating in front of them.

## 4. Pets

Chance of a pet: 7%, +2 per point of Warmth, −4 per point of Scarcity above 1, +2 for Head or
Warden, held to **0–14%**. Of those, chance of an exotic one, rolled separately: 2%, +2 per
rank, +2 per point of Strangeness, + Openness, held to **0–10%**. Uncanny animals only where
Strangeness is 2 or more.

A pet has a way of being kept (dotes on it, talks to it more than to people, keeps it for
someone who is gone…) and a loyalty. It is a tie (a **charge** at loyalty 3+), so harm to it
calls up tending, and its loss is a real grief. The hollow keep animals as working animals.
Someone who dislikes animals and keeps one anyway keeps it "despite".

## 5. Outlook on the unseen

Setting-agnostic: no gods are named. Express it through the setting's own faiths, or none.

**What they hold about how things ultimately are** (one):

| View | |
|---|---|
| Eternal | the self was before birth and will be after death |
| Partial | some things last forever; the rest of us pass |
| Measure | turns over whether the world has an edge, none, both or neither |
| Evasive | won't say yes, no, both or neither; every answer slippery |
| Chance | everything arose by chance, without cause or purpose |
| Aware | something of us goes on after death, aware |
| Unaware | something goes on, without awareness |
| Between | something goes on, neither aware nor unaware |
| Nothing | death is the end; after it, nothing |
| Here | whatever good there is must be found in this life, now |
| Unexamined | never thought about it; holds what they were taught |

**What they think happens at death** follows from the view: nothing; goes on aware; returns
in another life; dissolves into everything; is weighed by their deeds; rests without knowing;
lingers faintly; doesn't know (and it troubles them, or it doesn't); whatever they were taught.

**What it is for, to them:** themselves (safety, reward, escape); others (doing right, easing
hurt); their own people; no line between self and other at all; a bargain with the unseen;
lived without a name, and won't take one; nothing in particular.

**Practice:** none, small private habits, observant, devout, ascetic, secret, or outward only.
**Certainty:** doubting, wavering, settled, unshakeable. **Toward others' beliefs:** private,
tolerant, curious, persuading, scornful.

Leanings, not rules: a secular world tilts toward nothing, chance and the unexamined; a fervent
one toward the aware and the devout. The creed of Nothing and the Predator lean toward death
as the end; the Zealot toward certainty. Nobody is devout in a belief in nothing. The evasive
are never sure. The hollow perform belief and hold none.

## 6. Grief, by bond

Grief length starts from the role (a child longest), then this person's own pace, then how they
felt about who was lost, as a range rolled each time:

| Bond | Share of the usual grief |
|---|---|
| close | 0.9–1.4 |
| fond | 0.7–1.15 |
| dutiful | 0.5–0.95 |
| distant | 0.2–0.7 |
| strained | 0.3–0.9 |
| estranged | 0–0.5 (0–0.3 for a child they cut off or hated) |

A child they loved is never a short grief: at least 18–30 days, whatever their pace; a dutifully
loved child at least 9–18. Only an estranged bond can come to no grief at all ("if anything,
relief"). But 1 in 5 cold bonds hits harder once it can never be mended (1.0–1.6).

Their outlook colours how the grief shows: "talks of seeing them again", "watches newborns for
them", "takes no comfort in what comes after", "doesn't know where they've gone, and that is the
worst of it". Losing a child or partner can shake what they believe (half the time, one step of
certainty; never the unshakeable).

## 7. In play

- `meet --taste "rain"`: something in the scene touches a taste. Like +1, loved +2, disliked −1,
  loathed −2, on their side of the mood. Show it in the body or a word; never explain it.
- `meet --spirit shared|scorned`: the other party shares or scorns what they hold. Shared +1 if
  they are sure of it or try to persuade; scorned −1, −2 if unshakeable or devout; curious people
  take scorn more lightly; it does nothing to those for whom it does nothing, the evasive, or the
  hollow.
- Their outlook is shown to the narrator in solemn and grieving scenes.
- Days that bring or deny a small pleasure name one they actually like.
- `event … loss --about "their mother"` (any family label, or the pet's) marks them dead.
- `tie … --role pet` for a pet the story adds.
- Family, tastes and outlook are narrator-only. They come out through behaviour, talk and
  choices, never as a list.
