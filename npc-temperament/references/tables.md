## 1. Stations (d10) — how they hold and give

| d10 | Station | Gives by | Guard | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Head** — runs something (a household, crew, guild, division) | command | +1 | 3 |
| 2 | **Warden** — holds and defends a place or thing | guarding | +1 | 2 |
| 3–4 | **Steward** — manages goods or services for others | trade | 0 | 1 |
| 5 | **Herald** — carries word, knows things | telling | 0 | 1 |
| 6 | **Threshold** — keeps a gate, desk, checkpoint | letting pass | 0 | 1 |
| 7–8 | **Hand** — makes and works | making | 0 | 0 |
| 9 | **Sworn** — bound by a loyalty: oath, contract, crew, family, corp, gang | only within that loyalty | +1 | 2 |
| 10 | **Unmoored** — no fixed place | whim | −1 | 0 |

## 3. Ties (d3+1 per character)

Kind (d14): 1 kin · 2 lover · 3 friend · 4 mentor · 5 rival · 6 enemy · 7 creditor (they owe) ·
8 debtor (owes them) · 9 master or employer · 10 group · 11 place · 12 cause · 13 object ·
14 dependant.

Each tie has **Loyalty** (d6: 1 a small favour … 6 would die for it) and **Reach** (d6: how
much it can do in the world). Name the tie when the story needs it, then keep the name.

Kin ties are given a face from the character's family (life.md): their younger sister, their
father. The relative they hold dearest is added as a tie if not already one, and a pet, if they
keep one, is a tie of its own.

Enemies have what they would do (d6): 1 avoid them · 2 speak against them · 3 work against
them quietly · 4 hurt someone they love · 5 hurt them · 6 see them dead.

(blunt / roundabout) · 8 Light / Dark (cheerful / brooding) · 9 Good / Petty · 10 Square /

| d4 | Ratio | Day die | First side on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1:1 | d2 | 1 |
| 2 | 2:1 | d3 | 1–2 |
| 3 | 3:2 | d5 | 1–3 |

The side is rolled with the day and holds all day. Only one side shows per beat.

## 8. Oddities (1% per scene, one at most)

Ordinary oddities, any scene (d12): 1 gives a gift, then asks for it back next time ·
2 insults you while fixing something of yours · 3 helps your rival the same day they helped
you · 4 warns you off, then follows you · 5 swears they don't know you, in front of others ·
6 overpays you, then haggles a coin · 7 praises a thing they clearly hate · 8 weeps at good
news · 9 shuts the door, leaves food outside it · 10 tells a truth that harms them · 11 lies to
protect you, badly · 12 recounts your last meeting with the details wrong.

Uncanny oddities, only when Strangeness ≥ 2 (added to the pool): talking with no one when you
arrive · a place set for someone who never comes · every clock or screen near them wrong by
the same few minutes · repeats your last sentence back exactly, then carries on · animals or
machines nearby behave oddly around them · asks, sincerely, what year it is · carries an
object they don't remember getting · checks a door they locked, and it's unlocked.

No oddity ever reveals what a character couldn't know, or what the narrator knows.

## 9. Danger keywords

Any one in the scene brings that category's danger history into play.

| Category | Words |
|---|---|
| **violence** | weapon drawn, shot, blade, fight, a threat spoken aloud |
| **injury** | blood, body, corpse, wound, screaming |
| **disaster** | fire, smoke, explosion, flood, storm, collapse, quake |
| **authority** | armed guards, police, soldiers, security, a raid |
| **contagion** | plague, sickness, poison, quarantine |
| **uncanny** | something impossible witnessed, the dead moving, a voice from nowhere |

Treat anything clearly of a kind as that category, and add setting words freely.
