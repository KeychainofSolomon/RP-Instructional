## 1. Approaches

Judge the player's (or another character's) dominant approach in the scene. Each score comes
from the target's axes: add the two terms, halve, round toward zero, clamp to ±2.

| Approach | What it looks like | Score from |
|---|---|---|
| **Deference** | titles, bowing, letting them lead | Dominance − Candour |
| **Familiarity** | first names, closeness, touch | Energy + Warmth |
| **Bluntness** | straight talk, no padding | Candour − Volatility |
| **Flattery** | praise, admiration | Energy − Candour |
| **Haggling** | bargaining, terms | Energy − Warmth |
| **Humour** | jokes, lightness | Energy + Openness |
| **Patience** | silence, waiting, letting them come | Discipline − Energy |
| **Principle** | appeal to duty, ideals, what's right | Discipline − Openness |
| **Pity** | showing need or hurt, asking sympathy | Warmth + Volatility |
| **Pressure** | insisting, threatening | (−Volatility − Warmth) ÷ 2, −1, −1 more if Dominance ≥ 2 |
| **Generosity** | gifts, favours, bribes | 1 − Candour ÷ 2, −1 if Dominance ≥ 3 |

The vain warm to flattery and the sincere recoil. The warm are moved by pity, while the
cold-blooded take note of weakness. Pressure works on few people, mostly the cold and
unshakeable, who respect it. The approach score is a mood effect, not obedience: someone
who dislikes pressure may still give in out of fear, and their mood records what it cost.

Modifiers: starvation and inverted order (needs.md); Hollow characters score 0 on pity and
principle.

## 2. Creeds

Each person holds one value highest. The twelve sit on a circle. Neighbours get along;
opposites grind against each other.

```
 1 Strength   2 Gain      3 Excellence  4 Pleasure  5 Thrill    6 Freedom
 7 Mercy      8 Justice   9 Kin        10 Faith    11 Order    12 Security
```

Opposites (six apart): Strength–Mercy, Gain–Justice, Excellence–Kin, Pleasure–Faith,
Thrill–Order, Freedom–Security. A rare thirteenth, **Nothing**, sits outside the circle: it
clashes hard with Faith and is neutral to everything else.

Creed comes from the dominant voice (d6: 1–3 its first creed, 4–5 its second, 6 any).

**In play**:
- If the other party has shown, through deeds or words, that they serve the same or a
  neighbouring creed, mood +1. If they serve the opposite, −1.
- An appeal to Principle that lands on their creed gains +1 more; one that invokes the
  opposite creed loses 1 more.
- What people say and do is what's judged. The NPC doesn't read minds.

## 3. The room

Group scenes have their own dynamics. At scene start:
- **Lead**: the person with the highest Dominance + Energy + station rank (Head 3; Warden,
  Sworn 2; Steward, Herald, Threshold 1; Hand, Unmoored 0).
- **Social proof**: people who defer (Dominance ≤ −1 or Discipline ≥ 2) take ±1 toward the
  lead's attitude to the newcomer.
- **Rivals of the lead** take ±1 against it ("anyone she hates can't be all bad").
- **Contagion**: the loudest person (highest Energy, then Dominance) sets a feeling in the
  room. Everyone with Volatility ≥ 1 moves ±1 toward that band, once per scene.
- **Audience**: the Dramatic and Self-Assured play to it; the Sensitive and Solitary
  withdraw from it. Shifting characters wear their public face in front of a crowd.
  Masks slip more in private than in public.
- **Caps**: one ask, one hidden turn and one oddity per scene, for the whole room.

## 4. Regard between characters

Characters relate to each other through the same engine. Starting regard between two people:

| Part | Score |
|---|---|
| Creeds | same +2 · neighbours +1 · five apart −1 · opposites −2 (Nothing vs Faith −2; Nothing vs Nothing +1) |
| Dominance | both ≥ 2: −1 (rivals) · one ≥ 2 and the other ≤ −1: +1 (they fit) |
| Warmth | combined ≥ 3: +1 · either ≤ −2: −1 |
| Candour gap ≥ 4 | −1 (the honest distrust the slippery) |
| Same station, both weight 3 on Means or Standing | −1 (competing for the same thing) |
| Tie | lover +3 · kin, friend +2 · mentor, dependant +1 · creditor −1 · rival −2 · enemy −4 |

Clamp to ±5 and use as trust between them. Each still reacts through their own mood and
manner, so a warm rivalry and a cold alliance are both possible.

## 5. Circles

A circle (household, crew, gang, guild, house, cult, corporation) is built **exactly like a
person**: axes, voices, needs, creed, authenticity, drive, ties. Build it from its members
(axes and needs averaged, weighted by station rank + 1; its voices are its members' voices,
weighted the same way; its creed is the most common one), or roll it fresh for a faceless
institution.

Because it has the same shape, everything works at that scale:
- It rolls days. A good or bad day for the circle echoes at half strength into each member's
  same rung.
- It has a leading voice (a faction) that shifts with its hungers.
- It can be Masked (a public face of charity over a core of profit) or Hollow (a body going
  through motions nobody believes in any more).
- It has regard toward outsiders: a **reputation**, which works as trust for members meeting
  someone for the first time.
- Members with Discipline ≥ 2 or Dominance ≤ −1 lean toward the circle's view of an outsider
  by 1. The independent-minded don't.

Circles sit inside the tide of the times (time.md), which presses on the circle, which presses
on the person.

## 6. Conflict

Hostility doesn't mean attacking. When a character opposes someone, they choose a way of
opposing from what they *know* about the balance of strength.

**Own strength** = station rank + (best tie's reach ÷ 2) + ground (+1 on their own ground, −1
outnumbered or exposed). **Their estimate** of the other side comes only from what they've
seen and heard, so they can misjudge. Difference = own − estimate, then: +1 if Dominance ≥ 2,
−2 if Dominance ≤ −2, +1 if Volatility ≥ 2 and they're weaker (desperation), −1 if
Discipline ≥ 2 and they're stronger (patience).

| Difference | Stratagem |
|---|---|
| ≥ 3 | **Overwhelm**: confront openly, or toy with them |
| 1–2 | **Press**: demand terms, show strength |
| 0 | **Wait and probe**: test, seek allies, feint |
| −1 to −2 | **Avoid battle**: deceive, appear compliant, strike where unguarded |
| ≤ −3 | **Yield, flee, or appeal to a stronger power** |

Candour ≤ −2: prefers deception at any strength below overwhelming. The wise fight only when
they've already won; the weak win by not being where the blow falls. Let NPCs gather knowledge
before they act, through spies, gossip and watching, and let them act on what they've
gathered, never on what the narrator knows.

## 7. Receiving help

| Who | Response |
|---|---|
| Proud and principled (Dominance ≥ 2, Candour ≥ 1) | Refuses charity (−1). Accepts a fair trade. |
| Entitled (Candour ≤ −2, or Dominance ≥ 2 with Warmth ≤ 0) | Takes it as owed. No trust gained now. |
| Volatile and proud (Volatility ≥ 2, Dominance ≥ 1) | Accepts, and resents being helped. Warmth comes later, if at all. |
| Hollow | Performs gratitude. Trust moves only for real usefulness (rescue, material help). |
| Everyone else | Trust +1 (small help) or +2 (a life, a livelihood). |

Meeting a starving need counts double in what they remember, not in the number.

## 8. Codes and compulsions

At creation, d6: 1–3 none, 4–5 one, 6 two; each is a code or a compulsion (d2), with a
control rating of 6, 9, 12 or 15 (d4): how well they hold to the code, or hold off the urge.

- **Compulsions** build up. Each day there's a 1 in 6 chance of temptation crossing their
  path, and every trigger in a scene counts too. When temptations reach control ÷ 3, they act
  on it at the next real opportunity, then the count resets. Nothing is rolled at the moment
  of choice: the build-up decided it.
- **Codes** hold unless a Starved need with weight greater than control ÷ 4 directly
  conflicts with them. A code with control 15 never breaks that way. Breaking a code starves
  their Selfhood and is never forgotten.

## 9. Relationships and processing

Ties can name a specific person or thing and a **role**:
`forge.py tie maren.json --to "Bram" --role sibling --loyalty 5`. Roles: child, parent, sibling,
spouse, lover, kin, friend, dependant, student, mentor, spiritual, master, subordinate, follower,
comrade, rival, enemy, creditor, debtor, symbol. Each maps to a class (beloved, charge, revered,
authority, familiar, rival, enemy, conceptual). Kin and friends count as beloved at loyalty 4+.

Relationships decide **violence responses** (violence.md) and **grief** (time.md): who was hurt
or lost, and who did it, change everything. A tending type doesn't go to anyone wounded: they go
to their own, and to strangers only if very warm or merciful.

**Processing** is how a person takes what happens to them, worked out from their traits: Numb,
Expressive, Devotional, Action, Contained, Caretaking, Intellectual, Bargaining (Hollow people
perform). It shapes their response to violence, how grief shows ("keeps working; won't stop
working", "talks as if it can still be undone", "looks after everyone else"), and when it
arrives: the numb grieve late, after a few days of carrying on as if nothing happened.
