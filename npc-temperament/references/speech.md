# Speech

How people talk: a baseline manner that belongs to the person, bent by mood, pulled by the moment,
never replaced by either. All of it is worked out by `forge.py`; `meet` prints it each encounter
and `npc` shows the baseline on the sheet.

## 1. The dimensions

- **Register**: street, plain, formal, ceremonial.
- **Length**: terse, measured, expansive.
- **Warmth of address**: from the manner's warm turn to its cold turn, set by mood.
- **Directness and honesty**: how they bend words (section 4).
- **Figuration**: carried by the manner's habits: proverbs, aphorisms, jargon, scripture, images,
  stories, euphemism, numbers.

## 2. The 25 manners

Chosen at creation by weight: each manner fits certain traits (Candour, Volatility, Energy, Warmth,
Discipline, Openness, Dominance), certain voices, creeds and world settings. High station favours
the ceremonial; street manners grow rarer among the highly ranked. Hollow people lean toward the
performed manners (salon, corporate, velvet). In 45% of people a second manner blends in at 1/4,
2/5 or 1/2 (never more than two registers apart); its keywords colour the first, and one of its
habits shows up now and then.

## 3. Slang

Only street and plain speakers carry slang: street speakers two mechanisms, plain speakers half
the time one. The mechanisms are how slang is built in real languages everywhere; the narrator
invents the actual words for the setting, so it never ties the world to a real place.

- **reversal**: flips syllables or letters of ordinary words; the flipped word drifts to a new meaning.
- **rhyme**: says a phrase that rhymes with the word, then drops the rhyming half.
- **borrowing**: words taken from neighbouring tongues, the old country, or newcomers.
- **cant**: a dozen words for the watch, for money, for stealing; one for everything else.
- **figure**: a picture for a thing: windows for eyes, a fish for a fake.
- **suffix**: tacks a made-up ending onto ordinary words to make them theirs.
- **clipping**: cuts words short and runs them together.
- **codes**: numbers and initials that sound like words or stand for them.
- **tags**: ends lines with a tag that asks for agreement.
- **oaths**: swears by something holy or by their mother as a flourish, true or not.

A deceitful world, or a guarded person, favours cant and reversal (words that hide things from
outsiders). A scarce world favours borrowing and figures. Curious minds favour rhymes and codes.
**Code-switching**: disciplined or curious slang speakers (and some others) drop the street words
when the register is pulled up (in front of authority, at a ceremony) and pick them up again
among their own.

## 4. How they bend words

Worked out from Candour, the world's Deceit, the dominant voice (schemers and predators lean
manipulative) and whether they wear a face, with a small personal variance.

- **Plain**. Everyday: specific and concrete; says no plainly; admits what they don't know. Scheming: lays their reasons out, even the unflattering ones.
- **Tactful**. Everyday: true, but softened; leaves the hardest part unsaid. Scheming: picks the kindest true thing to say, and stops there.
- **Palterer**. Everyday: never quite lies; true things, arranged to mislead. Scheming: answers a different question, truthfully; lets the wrong impression stand.
- **Spinner**. Everyday: frames everything; flattery, what everyone else is doing, what you surely already agree. Scheming: builds the question so that every answer is yes.
- **Worker**. Everyday: works on you: obligation, guilt, a deadline that isn't real. Scheming: a small yes first, then the real ask; if caught, turns it round so that you're the one at fault.

The everyday form shows in any meeting; the scheming form shows when they have a stratagem
against someone or a face to keep. In an ordinary world about one person in fourteen works on
people; in a deceitful one, about one in six.

**Never a tell.** Real research on lying finds the cues small and unreliable; specific detail,
admitting what one doesn't remember, and self-correction lean truthful, but practised liars use
them too, and honest people flatter, hedge and soften. So none of this confirms or exposes
anything. Tells come only from a slip, at or after a deception (the no-leak rules), and nothing ever confirms
that someone is telling the truth.

## 5. The moment

- Register: up one step for solemn, superior, duty, public, tending; down one step for festive,
  intoxicated, intimate, exhausted, and for rage in the undisciplined. Code-switchers always go up
  for authority. Never more than one step from their baseline.
- Length: one step shorter in pain, fear, exhaustion, sickness, hunger, rage or shock; one step
  longer when festive or drunk (for anyone not low on energy). Frozen or stunned: terse.
- Habits of speech: two of the manner's three each encounter, so the same person never reads the
  same twice; the moment never strips them away.
- Punctuation sits on top (circumstance.md): exclamations, semicolons, the odd CAPITAL.

## 6. Audit

`audit` checks that there are at least twenty manners, every manner is complete, every manner,
bend and slang mechanism occurs across 3,000 built people, no manner exceeds 15%, the moment never
moves register more than a step or strips the habits, the frozen stay terse, and a neutral meeting
carries no warm or cold turn. Old ledgers get their speech worked out the first time they're used,
the same every time.
